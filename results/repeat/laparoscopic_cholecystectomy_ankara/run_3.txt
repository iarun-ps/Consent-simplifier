1. Information about Laporoscopic Cholecystectomy (surgery to remove the gallbladder):
- You have a gallbladder problem due to stones. There is no other treatment.
- If you don't have the surgery, your symptoms will get worse over time.
- If you have gallstones blocking the main cystic duct, you may get jaundice.

Laporoscopic Cholecystectomy is a surgery that removes the gallbladder through 4 small cuts in the abdomen.

- After the surgery, your symptoms like abdominal pain, indigestion, and bloating usually go away.

2. Risks of Laporoscopic Surgery:
• Organ injury
• Major vessel injury
• Leakage of gas to the subcutaneous tissue and chest cavity
• Leakage of gas to vessels

3. Risks of Laporoscopic Cholecystectomy:
• There is a 3% chance you will need to switch to open surgery.
• The main cystic duct may be tied, lacerated, or damaged.
• Vessels that supply the liver may be damaged or cut.
• There may be massive bleeding from the liver.
• The gallbladder may be perforated, and bile and gallstones may spill to the abdominal cavity.
• Other conditions in the abdominal cavity may not be found.
• It may not be possible to remove the gallbladder completely and may drain.

4. After the Surgery:
• There may be narrowing or obstruction of the main cystic duct.
• Bile may leak from the surgical site, and an abscess may develop.
• Excessive bleeding may require a corrective surgery.
• The supply of the liver may be impaired.
• Gallstones that fall into the abdominal cavity may develop into an abscess if not removed.
• There may be bleeding, inflammation, or abdominal wall hernia from the trocar site.
• A post-cholecystecomy syndrome may develop (indigestion and postprandial pain)
• If converted to open surgery, infection or hernia may develop from the incision (a cut made during surgery) site.
