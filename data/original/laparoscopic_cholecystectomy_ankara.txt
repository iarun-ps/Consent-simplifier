1. INFORMATION RELATED TO LAPAROSCOPIC CHOLECYSTECTOMY:
Acute or chronic cholelithiasis is inflammation of the gall bladder due to the presence of one or multiple stones. The only treatment available for this condition. There are no alternative treatment options available. The success rate of the surgery is more than 95%. If left untreated, patient's symptoms worsen over time. Obstruction of the main cystic duct with gallstones may result in jaundice.

LAPAROSCOPIC CHOLECYSTECTOMY is the surgical removal of the gallbladder from 4 small ports that are opened in the abdominal cavity and is also known as the closed method.

Patient's symptoms including abdominal pain, indigestion, distention, usually resolve after the surgery.

2. COMPLICATIONS SPECIFIC TO LAPAROSCOPIC SURGERY:
2.1. Organ injury
2.2. Major vessel injury
2.3. Leakage of gas to the subcutaneous tissue and chest cavity
2.4. Leakage of gas to vessels

3. COMPLICATIONS ASSOCIATED WITH LAPAROSCOPIC CHOLECYSTECTOMY:
3.1. Depending on technical difficulties, structural problems, and stage of the disease, there is 3% conversion to open surgery.
3.2. The main cystic duct may be tied, lacerated, or damaged.
3.3. Vessels that supply the liver may be damaged or cut.
3.4. There may be massive bleeding from the liver.
3.5. The gallbladder may be perforated, bile and gallstones may spill to the abdominal cavity.
3.6. Inside the abdominal cavity, other conditions not included in the visual field may not be identified.
3.7. It may not be possible to remove the gallbladder completely and may drain.

4. AFTER THE SURGERY:
4.1. There may be narrowing or obstruction of the main cystic duct.
4.2. Bile may leak from the surgical site and an abscess may develop.
4.3. Excessive bleeding may require a corrective surgery.
4.4. The supply of the liver may be impaired.
4.5. Gallstones that fall into the abdominal cavity may develop into an abscess if not removed.
4.6. There may be bleeding, inflammation, or abdominal wall hernia from the trocar site.
4.7. A post-cholecystecomy syndrome may develop (indigestion and postprandial pain)
4.8. If converted to open surgery, infection or hernia may develop from the incision site.
